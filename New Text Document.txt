🎲 How D&D Dice Rolls Work
Most of the time in Dungeons & Dragons (5e and most systems), the outcomes of actions—attacks, spells, saving throws, ability checks—are determined by rolling a d20 (a 20-sided die) and adding your relevant stat modifier.

📏 Here's the basic math:
You roll a d20

You add a modifier (usually from an ability score like STR or WIS, plus proficiency if it applies)

You compare the total to a Difficulty Class (DC)

If your result meets or beats the DC, you succeed.
If it's lower than the DC, you fail.

🎯 Example: Wisdom Save vs DC 18
Let’s say Kaelen has a +2 modifier to WIS:

You roll a 17

Add +2 → total = 19

That would succeed

But if he only had +1, total = 18, and he just passes

In your recent roll, if Kaelen rolled 17 and has +0 or +1, he just missed the DC 18.

😫 Why Does It Feel Like You Fail So Often?
Here are some possible reasons:

1. High DCs
The challenges in your campaign are scaling with the story: god-touched beings, divine roots, city-wide spells. That means DCs are often 16–18+. Those are hard rolls, and require high modifiers or luck.

2. No Advantage Mechanic Yet
You're not often using advantage (rolling two d20s and taking the higher)—which improves success chance significantly. Most people succeed ~70% of the time with advantage, but only ~30–40% on a flat d20 vs DC 18.

3. D20 Is Swingy
A 20-sided die has a lot of variance. It's a very random system—great for drama, terrible for reliability. You’re just experiencing bad variance (i.e., streaks of below-average rolls).

🔧 Tips to Improve Roll Odds:
Use skills you're good at (high modifiers)

Gain advantage through help, spells, or positioning

Collect inspiration or Memory Echo Tokens (like your party has) to reroll failures

Choose abilities or items that force rerolls or lower DCs
